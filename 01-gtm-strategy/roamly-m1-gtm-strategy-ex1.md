# Roamly Groups · GTM Strategy, Module 1 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **What is the core business problem Roamly is trying to solve with Roamly Groups?:** Roamly is built for solo bookings. Groups are already forcing workarounds (separate accounts, manual payment splits, manual syncing), causing high drop-off on the highest-value bookings.
- **What does success look like if this launch goes well? Think beyond revenue, the product, the brand, and the customer relationship.:** Group trips become a referral engine (one organizer brings several new users). Repeat usage deepens.
- **What signals suggest this is a real market opportunity worth pursuing? What is the behavioral data telling you about unmet demand?:** Support tickets about group coordination. People aren't waiting to be asked, they're already attempting this workaround today. High drop-off on multi-traveler sessions
- **List your top 5 possible segments that could benefit from Roamly Groups.:** 1. Families
2. Friends group
3. Small team/companies offsite
4. Celebration/parties/weddings
5. Travel community meetup/group
- **How might you size the market? Think industry reports, travel research, social listening, competitor analysis.:** 1. Internal data: multiple separate bookings for the same session, made close together in time, often from accounts sharing a payment method or IP/device.
2. Industry report
3. Competition analysis like splitwise, tripIT, 
4. Social listening
- **Which cross-functional stakeholders would you include to validate and execute? Think beyond product and engineering, who has context on the customer, market, and commercial model?:** Product, engineering, finance (split payments, fraud exposure), host ops (group-size support), customer support (source of the original signal), marketing/growth (referral loop design), legal/trust & safety (liability across group members).
- **Which one segment is the most valuable to target first, and why?:** 1. First segment: Friend groups planning a trip together.
- **What is the core value proposition for that segment?:** Plan it together, book it once." One organizer sets up the trip, the group sees one itinerary, payment splits automatically.
