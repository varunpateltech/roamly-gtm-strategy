## 1. The Discover Framework

_Applied to Roamly Groups, in the order taught: Goal → Research → Team → Hypothesis._

**Step 1 · Align on the Goal**
- **Core problem:** Roamly is built for solo bookings. Groups are already forcing workarounds (separate accounts, manual payment splits, manual syncing), causing high drop-off on the highest-value bookings.
- **Success beyond revenue:** Group trips become a referral engine, one organizer brings several new users. Repeat usage deepens, and a support complaint turns into a reason to choose Roamly.

**Step 2 · Do the Research**
- **Demand signals:** Support tickets about group coordination, high drop-off on multi-traveler sessions, clustered individual bookings for the same shared-capacity session, and a 12,000-host network already used informally for group-sized experiences.
- **Top 5 candidate segments:** Friend groups, families, small team/company offsites, celebrations/parties/weddings, travel-community meetup groups.
- **Market sizing approach:** internal signals (clustered bookings, support ticket volume), industry group-travel reports, competitor analysis (Splitwise, TripIt), social listening for group-booking complaints.

**Step 3 · Build a Working Team**
- Product, engineering, finance (split payments, fraud exposure), host ops (group-size support), customer support (source of the original signal), marketing/growth (referral loop design), legal/trust & safety (liability across group members).

**Step 4 · Form Your Hypothesis**
- **First segment:** Friend groups planning a trip together, most acute pain, no formal tooling exists for them today, and each group brings multiple new users.
- **Core value proposition:** "Plan it together, book it once." One organizer sets up the trip, the group sees one itinerary, payment splits automatically.

## 2. The problem & the audience

- **Problem worth solving:** Coordinating a group trip on Roamly today means separate accounts, manual payment splitting, and manually syncing availability, friction high enough to cause visible drop-off on the highest-intent bookings.
- **Primary ICP:** An organizer within a friend group (not a title/role, a behavior) who is planning a shared experience for their group and currently manages it manually across chat apps, spreadsheets, and Venmo.
- **What they do today (the alternative):** Book separately across individual accounts, split costs manually after the fact, and coordinate availability outside the platform (group chats, spreadsheets).

## 3. GTM motion

**Chosen motion:** Hybrid, **PLG primary, CLG secondary**.

_Why this motion:_ All five diagnostic inputs point to PLG, time to value is minutes, the same person books and uses it, annual value is low/one-time, the sales cycle is self-serve, and it scales via product virality (organizer invites bring new users). CLG layers on top because group travel decisions are peer-influenced and get shared inside existing travel communities, amplifying the PLG invite loop rather than replacing it.

- **Acquisition:** Organizer starts a booking and invites the group via a shareable link, each invite is an acquisition event for Roamly.
- **Activation:** Group members join the shared itinerary and complete their share of payment, the "aha" moment is seeing the whole trip and cost split resolved in one place.
- **Monetization:** Standard booking revenue, captured earlier in the funnel than today because the group no longer drops off due to coordination friction.

## 4. V1 one-pager

> For friend groups planning a trip together, who currently coordinate bookings through separate accounts and manual payment splitting, Roamly Groups is a group-booking feature that lets one organizer plan, invite, and split payment for the whole trip in one place. Unlike manual workarounds (spreadsheets, Venmo, group chats), Roamly Groups turns Roamly's existing trust (vetted hosts, 3x repeat bookings) into a reason to book as a group, not just solo.

**Key messages:**
1. Plan it together, book it once
2. No more chasing friends for money
3. Trusted hosts, now for your whole group

**Channel approach:** PLG-led, activation lives inside the product (organizer invites via shareable link), amplified by CLG (travel-community shares, power-user advocates). No paid acquisition needed for V1, the invite loop is the channel.

## 6. The assumption I'm most worried about

**Assumption:** That the organizer's social risk (vouching for Roamly to their friend group) is a blocker to adoption.

**Pushback / why I don't think it kills the strategy:** The organizer already trusts their own group before Roamly enters the picture, the risk is picking an activity, not vouching for a stranger. Roamly's vetted-host ratings and automatic split payments reduce the organizer's risk versus today's manual workaround, where they personally front money and chase repayment. I'd test this by measuring organizer drop-off at the "invite the group" step in early usage, if organizers hesitate there, the trust concern is real and needs addressing (e.g., a preview/share-before-committing flow).
