# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **Pricing goal (Where to win first):** Entering a new market.
- **Why (Justification):** Roamly Groups is a new audience (groups, not solo travelers) with no existing price or track record. The goal is Acquire, and the north star is invite-to-signup rate, both about winning new users, not extracting more revenue from people already on Roamly. Charging for market entry now would work against the exact acquisition loop the motion depends on.
- **Pricing model (The model):** Transaction-based. A fee per completed group booking, not a subscription. The value is event-driven, a group forms, plans, and books once per trip or activity, not a continuous relationship with the pricing. This fits the PLG motion (self-serve, scales via virality, no sales cycle): a subscription would add friction right at the moment signups need to convert fast.
- **Who pays (Buyer vs. user):** No single buyer separate from the user, each group member pays their own share of the same booking, matching the positioning statement ("everyone confirms and pays their own share"). The fee itself could be a small add-on split across all shares, or built into Roamly's existing take rate on the booking.
- **Packaging (The structure):** One structure, but only one of the two audiences interacts with it. The organizer is the one who picks the tier, free core or a paid add-on like cancellation flexibility or priority booking. The invited member never makes that choice, they only see the outcome of what the organizer already picked, their own share to confirm and pay. So the organizer needs to see the tiers themselves to decide what to add. The invited member needs to see only their confirmed share, not a pricing menu at all.
- **Tiers (What each is for):** Good-Better-Best, applied on top of the core booking, not gating it.
  - **GOOD (free):** Core group booking, invite, shared plan, split payment. Every group. Most customers routed here.
  - **BETTER ($ add-on):** Flexible cancellation for the whole group, automatic payment reminders to anyone who hasn't confirmed. For organizers of larger or pricier trips who want less risk of one person stalling the group.
  - **BEST ($$ add-on):** Priority host booking for popular experiences, group-size discounts. For repeat organizers who plan trips often.
  - GOOD stays free because the pricing goal is entering a new market and the motion is PLG: charging for the core loop would tax the exact invite behavior that brings new users in.
- **Which research method (Van Westendorp, Max-Diff, Conjoint) would you commission, and what specific assumption are you trying to validate?:** Van Westendorp, built for a new product or feature with no starting price range yet. It tests the assumption that the core group booking should stay free, with only the add-ons priced. That assumption was a judgment call in the packaging decision, not evidence.
- **What is the one thing in your recommendation that would change everything if you got it wrong?:** If Van Westendorp shows organizers do have real willingness to pay for the core booking itself, not just the add-ons, then the whole Good-Better-Best structure is leaving revenue on the table from day one, and the free-core assumption needs to be rebuilt, not just adjusted.
