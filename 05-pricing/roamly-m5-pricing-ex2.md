### Roamly Groups — Pricing & Packaging Recommendation

**The call:** Enter the group-booking market with a transaction-based fee, no subscription, and keep the core coordination experience free. Monetize through optional add-ons layered on top.

**Why this goal now:** Roamly Groups is a new audience with no existing price or track record. The priority is bringing new users in through group invites, not extracting revenue from people already on the platform. A price on the core experience would tax the exact behavior the growth loop depends on, an organizer inviting friends who have never used Roamly before.

**The model:** A fee per completed group booking, not a subscription. Value is event-driven: a group forms, plans, and books once per trip, so pricing follows that same event, not a recurring charge unrelated to it. The fee is built into Roamly's existing take rate on the booking, not a separate line item, so no group member sees an added charge for using the coordination tools themselves. Each group member pays only their own share of the booking they already agreed to.

**The packaging:** Good-Better-Best, but only the trip organizer ever sees or chooses a tier. The invited group member sees no pricing menu at all, only their own confirmed share.
- **Good (free):** invite, shared plan, split payment. Where most groups land.
- **Better (paid add-on):** flexible cancellation for the whole group, automatic reminders to anyone who hasn't confirmed.
- **Best (paid add-on):** priority booking on popular experiences, group-size discounts. For organizers who plan trips repeatedly.

**What to validate first:** Whether the core booking should really stay free. That's currently a judgment call, not evidence. Commission Van Westendorp research, the right method for a new product with no starting price, to test willingness to pay on the core experience itself, before the add-on tiers are finalized. If organizers turn out willing to pay for the core loop, the whole free-core assumption needs rebuilding, not adjusting.

**For Finance:** Revenue starts embedded in the existing take rate, not a new visible charge. Add-on attach rate, not core adoption, is the number to watch for incremental margin.

**For Sales Leadership:** The self-serve motion covers individual friend groups. Watch for repeat organizers booking large or high-value groups (weddings, corporate offsites), that volume is the signal this stops being purely self-serve and needs a sales-assisted path.

**For Product Marketing:** The free core is the story, "nobody chased anyone for money," not a discount message. Frame add-ons as optional peace of mind, never as a paywall on the core promise.

**Single assumption to validate first, and how:** That the core group booking should stay free. Validate with Van Westendorp research on trip organizers, asking the four standard price-perception questions about the core coordination experience itself, before committing the tier structure to launch.

- **After reviewing the AI output, what did you change, and why?:** Two changes. First, the pricing model originally offered two different mechanisms for the fee, a visible add-on or an embedded take rate, without picking one. I picked the embedded take rate, since a stakeholder memo can't leave Finance guessing which mechanism is actually being proposed. Second, the Sales Leadership section originally just said "there's no negotiation here," which gives sales nothing to act on. I replaced it with a specific signal, repeat organizers booking large or high-value groups, so sales knows when a self-serve booking should be flagged for a different path.
