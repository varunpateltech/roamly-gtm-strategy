# Pricing & Packaging Recommendation — Roamly Groups

> Module 5 · Increase Revenue Through Smarter Pricing and Packaging — ★ Deliverable 5
>
> Propose a pricing model and packaging structure for a product with no existing price. Work in order: value first, packaging second, research last — then compile a one-page recommendation for stakeholders.

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Entering a new market.
- **Why this is the right commercial goal now:** Roamly Groups is a new audience with no existing price or track record. The strategy is built on winning new users through group invites, not extracting more revenue from people already on the platform. Charging for market entry now would work against the exact acquisition loop the motion depends on.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based.
- **Who pays, and for what?** No buyer separate from the end user. A fee per completed group booking, not a subscription, built into the existing take rate on the booking rather than a separate visible charge. Each group member pays only their own share of the booking itself, so the fee is never a line item any one person sees or feels.

Value is event-driven: a group forms, plans, and books once per trip, so pricing follows that same event, not a recurring charge unrelated to it. This fits a product-led motion that's self-serve with no sales cycle; a subscription would add friction right at the moment signups need to convert fast.

## 3. Packaging approach

- **Structure:** By features, applied as optional add-ons on top of a free core, not as gates on it.
- **Audiences & fit:** Two roles interact with the offering differently. The trip organizer is the one who picks a tier; the invited group member never makes that choice, they see only the outcome, their own confirmed share to pay. The organizer needs to see the tiers to decide what to add. The invited member needs to see only their share.

| Tier | Who it's for | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good | Every group | Free | Invite, shared plan, split payment |
| Better ★ | Organizers of larger or pricier trips | $ add-on | Flexible cancellation for the whole group, automatic reminders to anyone who hasn't confirmed |
| Best | Repeat organizers | $$ add-on | Priority booking on popular experiences, group-size discounts |

The free core is where most groups are routed, deliberately. Charging for the core loop would tax the exact invite behavior that brings new users in.

## 4. Research to validate

- **Method:** Van Westendorp, built for a new product or feature with no starting price range yet.
- **Assumption to validate:** That the core group booking should stay free, with only the add-ons priced. That's currently a judgment call, not evidence.
- **The one thing that would change everything if you got it wrong:** If organizers turn out to have real willingness to pay for the core booking itself, not just the add-ons, the whole packaging structure is leaving revenue on the table from day one, and the free-core assumption needs to be rebuilt, not adjusted.

## 5. The one-page recommendation

> We recommend a transaction-based fee with free-core, paid-add-on packaging for Roamly Groups, to enter the group-booking market, because the priority right now is winning new users through the invite loop, and any charge on the core experience would tax the exact behavior that loop depends on.

For each stakeholder, what they most need to hear:

- **Finance:** Revenue starts embedded in the existing take rate, not a new visible charge. Add-on attach rate, not core adoption, is the number to watch for incremental margin.
- **Sales Leadership:** The self-serve motion covers individual friend groups. Watch for repeat organizers booking large or high-value groups, such as weddings or corporate offsites, that volume is the signal this stops being purely self-serve and needs a sales-assisted path.
- **Product Marketing:** The free core is the story, "nobody chased anyone for money," not a discount message. Frame add-ons as optional peace of mind, never as a paywall on the core promise.

**Validate first:** Whether the core booking should really stay free. Commission Van Westendorp research on trip organizers, asking the four standard price-perception questions about the core coordination experience itself, before the tier structure is committed to launch.
