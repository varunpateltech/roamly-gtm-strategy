ROAMLY GROUPS · SEPTEMBER 2026
01 · GTM Strategy

A hybrid motion built for one segment's real friction

Friend groups planning a trip together, coordinating today across separate accounts, manual payment splitting, and manual availability syncing, friction visible enough to cause drop-off on the highest-intent bookings.

Motion & rationale

Hybrid: PLG primary, CLG secondary. Same person books and uses it, self-serve, low one-time value, scales via invite virality. Community layers on top since group travel decisions are peer-influenced.

Goal & target

Acquire. Each group invite is a net-new-user event. Target: the organizer within a friend group who manages bookings manually across chat apps, spreadsheets, and Venmo.

One-pager

"Plan it together, book it once." One organizer, one shared itinerary, everyone pays their own share automatically.

02 · Competitive Intel & Strategic Bet

Wanderlog owns planning. We own the booking.

The primary competitor is Wanderlog, a free group trip-planning app: purpose-built coordination UX, no curated host network, no in-app booking or payment.

Battlecard — why we win
  • Turns a shared plan into a booked, paid activity in one place; Wanderlog stops at the plan
  • Removes manual coordination (chat + spreadsheets + Venmo) at the exact step planning tools can't automate
Where they win
  • Free-form planning across a whole trip (flights, lodging, restaurants), not just activities
The strategic bet

"We are betting that Roamly Groups wins when a group has already decided what they want to do together and just needs to book and pay for it painlessly, because that's the exact moment free planning tools stop being useful and friction is highest."

Deprioritizing: broad itinerary-building features, in favor of deepening booking + split-payment.

03 · Positioning

Group booking for curated experiences, owned by trust

Positioning statement

"Roamly Groups is group booking for curated local experiences with vetted hosts: one friend organizing a trip invites the group, and everyone confirms and pays their own share in one shared booking. The group goes from ‘we should do this’ to booked and paid, without one person acting as planner and payment collector."

One-liner

"Plan it together, book it once."

Pressure-tested

A blind read showed the audience read broader than intended. Fixed by anchoring "one friend organizing a trip" so the travel and friend-group audience carries through.

05 · Pricing Recommendation

Free core, paid add-ons, priced to protect the invite loop

Transaction-based, built into the existing take rate, no separate charge any one person sees. Goal: enter the market, not maximize revenue on day one.

06 · Individual Insights

What building this actually taught me

Friction

Pricing the core booking. I kept defaulting to charging for coordination itself, until I separated what Roamly charges for from what the group pays each other.

Biggest "aha"

Researching the primary competitor showed it already owns planning, it just can't book or take payment. Roamly doesn't need to out-plan anyone, it needs to finish the job.

Key takeaway

A fuzzy call early on doesn't stay contained, it resurfaces later as someone else's problem. Strategy, positioning, messaging, and pricing are one decision, repeated in different formats.

What I'd do differently · Monday idea

Run the pricing research before locking packaging, not after. And before any pricing call: name explicitly which audience actually sees and chooses each part of it.
