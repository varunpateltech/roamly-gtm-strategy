# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2 (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Who is Roamly Groups actually for, based on everything you now know?:** Friend groups planning a trip together, who need to book and pay for shared activities without manual coordination, not full end-to-end trip planners.
- **What is the one thing that makes it worth choosing over the competitor?:** Roamly Groups books and settles payment in the same place; Wanderlog stops at the plan.
- **What is the biggest threat to that position, based on your battlecard?:** If Wanderlog adds booking/payment, or partners with a marketplace, they close the gap fast since they already own the coordination UX and user base.
- **Of all the signals you’re seeing, which one changes how you think about the market? Hint: looking at how your primary competitor wins, what does that tell you about what Roamly Groups needs to do differently to take that buyer?:** Wanderlog wins by owning the planning layer; Roamly wins by owning the booking + payment layer. Coordination tools and booking tools solve different halves of the same trip, the real opportunity is being the half that actually converts to revenue and resolved logistics.
- **Complete the sentence: “I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when because”:** I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when a group has already decided what they want to do together and just needs to book and pay for it painlessly, because that's the exact moment free planning tools stop being useful and friction (and drop-off) is highest.
- **“I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by”, your strategic commitment, not what you’re building but how you’ll win with it.:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by being the fastest, most trustworthy way for a group to turn a shared plan into a booked, paid activity, not by trying to out-plan dedicated planning apps.
- **“To make this bet, I am deprioritizing”, every strategic bet means giving something up. Naming the sacrifice is the proof a real decision was made.:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing broad itinerary-building features in favor of deepening the booking + split-payment experience for activities specifically.
