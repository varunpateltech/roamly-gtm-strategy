## 2. Battlecard — Roamly Groups vs. Wanderlog

**Know the Landscape**
- Wanderlog: free/freemium collaborative trip-planning app, no marketplace or host network
- Core offering: shared itineraries, collaborative maps, expense splitting
- Pricing: free tier + Wanderlog Pro subscription (offline maps, unlimited trips, ad-free)

**Understand the Battlefield**
- **Their strengths:** purpose-built group coordination UX, large organic trip-planner base, free to start
- **Their weaknesses:** no vetted host network, no in-app booking or payment, no trust layer on who you're paying or booking with

**Win the Deal**
- **Our one-line advantage:** Roamly Groups turns a shared plan into a booked, paid activity in one place; Wanderlog stops at the plan.
- **Why they win:** if a group's priority is free-form planning across the whole trip (flights, lodging, restaurants), not just activities, Wanderlog's flexibility beats Roamly's narrower scope
- **Why us over doing nothing:** groups already default to manual coordination (chat + spreadsheets); we remove that manual work at the exact step, booking and paying, that free planning tools can't automate

**Handle the Hard Moments**
- **Quick dismiss:** "Wanderlog helps you plan a trip. Roamly Groups helps you actually book and pay for it together, without switching apps."
- **Trap to set:** "Who's actually going to collect the money and confirm the booking once everyone's agreed on the plan?"
- **Objection → response:**
  - _"Wanderlog is free, why would we pay for Roamly?"_ → You're not paying for the planning, you're paying for the experience you'd book anyway, just without the manual coordination tax.
  - _"Can't we just use Wanderlog and Venmo together?"_ → You can, and that's exactly the friction (two apps, manual reconciliation) that causes the drop-off we're solving.

## 3. The strategic bet (Data → Insight → Belief → Bet)

**Data**
- Roamly Groups is for friend groups who need to book and pay for shared activities without manual coordination, not full end-to-end trip planners
- What makes it worth choosing over Wanderlog: we book and settle payment in the same place; Wanderlog stops at the plan
- Biggest threat: if Wanderlog adds booking/payment, or partners with a marketplace, they close the gap fast since they already own the coordination UX and user base

**Insight**
Wanderlog wins by owning the *planning* layer; Roamly wins by owning the *booking + payment* layer. Coordination tools and booking tools solve different halves of the same trip, the real opportunity is being the half that actually converts to revenue and resolved logistics.

**Belief**
> We are betting that Roamly Groups wins when a group has already decided what they want to do together and just needs to book and pay for it painlessly, because that's the exact moment free planning tools stop being useful and friction (and drop-off) is highest.

- **Why now:** support data already shows groups abandoning at this exact step (manual booking/payment coordination), the gap is proven, not hypothetical
- **What we're deliberately NOT doing:** building broad itinerary-planning features (flights, lodging, day-by-day scheduling) to compete with dedicated planners like Wanderlog
- **What would prove the bet right within 90 days:** a measurable drop in group-booking drop-off rate, and organic group invites converting to new signups at a healthy rate

**Bet**
- "I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by being the fastest, most trustworthy way for a group to turn a shared plan into a booked, paid activity, not by trying to out-plan dedicated planning apps."
- "To make this bet, I am deprioritizing broad itinerary-building features in favor of deepening the booking + split-payment experience for activities specifically."

## 4. Evidence

- Support tickets already document users manually coordinating group bookings across separate accounts and payments (Module 1 research)
- High drop-off rates on multi-traveler sessions, the friction is measurable, not assumed
- Wanderlog's own product scope (planning only, no booking/payment) confirms the market has split these two jobs apart, nobody has combined them yet for group experience booking

## Adjustment carried from this module

Revisiting the M1 value proposition: narrowing "shared itinerary planning" to **"one shared plan for your group's activities"**, honest about scope, and sharper against planning-only competitors like Wanderlog.
