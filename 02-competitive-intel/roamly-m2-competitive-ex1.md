# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **Competitor’s name:** Wanderlog
- **Their core offering:** Collaborative trip-planning app, shared itineraries, maps/routes, expense splitting
- **How they position themselves:** "The free trip planner" — an all-in-one group trip organizer
- **Their primary audience:** Friend groups, couples, budget-conscious travelers planning trips together
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Freemium, free tier + Wanderlog Pro subscription (offline maps, unlimited trips, no ads)
- **Why they’re your biggest threat:** Already owns the exact coordination job (shared itinerary + expense split) that is Roamly Groups' core differentiator. No curated bookable marketplace yet, but if they add booking or partner with one, they compete directly on our differentiator, not just an adjacent category
- **Looking at this battlecard, what is the one thing that makes you want to go back and adjust your M1 strategy, and why? “I want to revisit [x] because”:** I want to revisit the 'shared itinerary planning' claim in my value proposition because it implies Roamly Groups owns the whole trip-planning job, when it actually only covers bookable activities. Wanderlog exposed that gap, groups doing full-trip coordination (flights, lodging, restaurants) will still need a separate tool alongside Roamly. I'd narrow the claim to 'one shared plan for your group's activities' so the promise matches what we actually deliver, and so the differentiation against planning-only tools like Wanderlog stays honest and clean.
